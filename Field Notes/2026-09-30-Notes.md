# Session 1

**10/1/26 / 8:00pm / location: Homelab**

**What I'm doing today:** Building the basic groundwork for the project and collecting the first sound samples from malware captures.

**Setup:** Kali VM in UTM. Two mappings so far, forest and coastal, both using synthesized sounds for now.

## Workflow

- Downloaded a capture from malware-traffic-analysis.net.
- Briefly analyzed the pcap to find the victim machine (needed its IP for the next step).
- Ran the script to convert the pcap to a soundscape.
- Command:
  ```
  python3 netscape.py ~/malware-pcaps/2026-02-28-traffic-analysis-exercise.pcap -m mappings/forest.json -o malware02_forest.wav --target 10.2.28.2 --speed=15
  ```
- Flags:
  - `-m` chooses the mapping
  - `-o` names the output file
  - `--target` is the victim IP (its outgoing traffic plays on the right, incoming plays on the left)
  - `--speed` compresses the 20+ minute capture to avoid the out-of-memory error my current setup throws due to length of most captures.

## Sources rendered this session

- `easy123_*` from *2026-02-28, "Easy as 123"*, victim `10.2.28.2`
- `kongtuke_*` from *2026-09-11, "KongTuke rebuke!"*, victim `10.9.11.135`

## What I heard/noticed

**kongtuke_coastal.wav:** Opens as the loudest and most crowded of all four. A near-constant
layer of gull cries fills most of the whole minute. it's hard to pick out anything underneath. Every
so often a sharp click cuts through, and there's a low lapping just before the busier bursts.
The first ten seconds are the densest, then it thins out around 0:25. Feels less like a beach
and more like a crowded aviary.


**kongtuke_forest.wav:** This is the same traffic but brighter and sharper. This mapping creates a more nervous feel for the listener. The twig snaps are more noticeable here than the clicks were in the coastal version. The wind bed is so faint I can barely tell it's there.


**easy123_coastal.wav:** Calmer and more spread out than KongTuke. There's space
between events and allows you time to digest what you're hearing. The low end is a fuller sound with more lapping and a surf sound. The bell also shows up more often.

This capture has gulls almost always present with regualar bells and water drops. Subtle wave activity. 1.33m has a series of water drops.

**easy123_forest.wav:** This one was the odd one out of the four. Instead of birdsong, it's has a lot of crackling
twig snaps which almost sound like a fire or radio crackle. Many of the bird chirps get buried under the crackle. 

This capture primarily has cricket chirps with water drops at consistent intervals. Extremely quiet wind.

## Decisions

- Used premade captures instead of generating my own attacks. Wanted to test on real-world malware traffic.
- Set `--target` to the victim so the infected machine's direction is audible in the stereo field.
- Used `--speed 15` because real-time renders ran out of memory. This speed feels a little bit too fast and skews sound. 
- With these initial samples, I mapped both samples to both soundscapes.
